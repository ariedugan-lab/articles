## The Numbers Behind the Signal

Shanghai Disneyland crossed 100 million cumulative visitors in 2025. That milestone came during a period when China's domestic consumption was under pressure, foreign investment sentiment was cautious, and many tourist-dependent businesses were tightening cash flow forecasts. Disney's ability to sustain attendance growth over a decade—without relying on a single blockbuster quarter—exposes a flaw in how most lenders evaluate businesses that serve large anchor attractions.

Standard underwriting for a hotel, tour operator, or food distributor near a major theme park typically relies on year-over-year revenue or foot traffic data. That approach captures seasonal spikes and dips. It does not capture the compounding effect of accumulated demand. A business that serves a park with a steadily growing cumulative visitor base has a fundamentally different risk profile than one tied to a venue with flat or volatile annual numbers. The difference is not marginal. It is structural.

## The Metric That Matters: Cumulative Attendance Velocity

Cumulative Attendance Velocity (CAV) measures the rate at which a destination's total visitor count increases over a multi-year period. Unlike annual visitation which can swing with weather, holidays, or local events, CAV smooths out noise. It reveals the underlying demand trajectory.

For Shanghai Disneyland, CAV averaged approximately 10 million new visitors per year since opening in 2016. The growth rate held steady even as China's GDP growth slowed and consumer sentiment dipped. That consistency is the signal lenders need. A merchant whose revenue correlates to CAV rather than single-year attendance has a more predictable repayment capacity.

**Implications for funders:** Replace or supplement annual revenue benchmarks with a CAV-linked metric when underwriting merchants that derive >30% of revenue from a specific anchor destination. Use the destination's cumulative growth rate to set advance rates. A hotel or transport provider with a 10-year CAV trend of 8% annual growth is safer than one with 15% year-over-year swings.

**Implications for brokers:** When evaluating a merchant's exposure to a major attraction, ask for the destination's cumulative attendance history. If the data shows steady growth through macro downturns, that merchant deserves a stronger credit profile. Use that to negotiate better terms for your client.

**Implications for ISOs:** Target businesses that serve the park's supply chain, not just tourist-facing operators. Food distributors, uniform suppliers, maintenance contractors, and waste haulers often have contracts tied to park attendance volumes. Their revenue streams are indirect but more stable because they are not subject to same-day consumer decisions. CAV becomes a leading indicator for their working capital needs.

## Why Year-Over-Year Data Fails

A typical underwriting file for a hotel near Shanghai Disneyland might show 2022 revenue down 40% from 2019. That looks like a distressed business. But the cumulative attendance data tells a different story: the park added 8 million visitors in 2022 despite COVID restrictions. The hotel's drop was due to temporary travel bans, not demand destruction. A lender using only annual comparisons would decline a loan. A lender using CAV would see the recovery trajectory and approve.

This is not hypothetical. Many tourism-adjacent small businesses carry fixed operating costs—leases, payroll, insurance—that do not adjust to short-term dips. Their working capital needs persist. A CAV-based model gives funders confidence to lend through the trough because the underlying demand engine is still accelerating.

## The Repeat Visitor Problem and Its Underwriting Solution

Cumulative attendance includes first-time and repeat visitors. The mix matters. A high repeat rate signals brand loyalty and reduces the risk that the attraction loses its appeal. Shanghai Disneyland's repeat rate is estimated at 30-35%, well above industry average for regional parks. That loyalty stabilizes revenue for adjacent businesses. A tour operator that serves repeat visitors can predict cash flow more accurately than one that depends on first-time tourists.

**Actionable step for underwriters:** Request the destination's repeat visitor rate. If it exceeds 25%, treat the merchant's revenue as more resilient. If repeat rates are low and growth is driven entirely by new visitors, the merchant is more exposed to first-impression risk—one bad review cycle can compress arrivals.

## The Ownership Factor: Joint Venture Stability

Shanghai Disneyland is a joint venture between Disney (43%) and Shanghai Shendi Group (57%), a state-owned enterprise. That ownership structure introduces unique dynamics. State backing provides capital stability and policy access. It also introduces regulatory and political dependencies that pure private attractions do not face. For a small business lender, this means the anchor destination's credit quality is tied to both a global brand and a local government entity.

**Underwriting rule:** When the anchor attraction involves a state-owned partner, adjust your risk premium downward for revenue stability but upward for potential sudden policy shifts (e.g., visa restrictions, holiday calendar changes). The net effect for most destination-adjacent businesses is a lower default rate but a higher tail-risk. Stress-test for a scenario where attendance growth halts for 12 months due to government action.

## How to Operationalize Cumulative Attendance Data

1. **Source the data.** Most major theme parks, national parks, and entertainment districts publish annual or quarterly attendance figures. For privately held attractions, use proxy data from vendor interviews, hotel occupancy tax reports, or transportation authority counts.

2. **Calculate CAV.** Take the last five years of cumulative attendance. Fit a linear trend. Use the slope as the growth rate. If the R-squared is above 0.8, the trend is reliable. Apply that rate to the merchant's revenue to estimate baseline cash flow.

3. **Apply a discount.** No single metric is perfect. Discount the CAV-based revenue projection by 15-25% to account for merchant-specific risks (management quality, lease terms, local competition).

4. **Monitor divergence.** If the merchant's revenue growth starts to decouple from the anchor attraction's CAV, investigate. The gap could signal internal mismanagement or a shift in the competitive landscape.

## A Concrete Example

A commercial laundry service that processes linens for four hotels near Shanghai Disneyland saw revenue drop 22% in 2020 and 18% in 2022. A traditional lender using year-over-year comparisons would flag the business as high risk. But the laundry's contract volumes tracked park attendance with a two-week lag. Cumulative attendance grew 9% annually through those same years. The laundry was not in decline. It was operating below capacity during temporary shocks. A lender using CAV would have offered a working capital facility to cover the dip, capturing a strong recovery in 2023-2025.

The merchant survived by drawing down personal savings. That is capital inefficient. A CAV-informed lender could have deployed funds at lower risk and earned yield through the cycle.

## The Broader Takeaway for Small Business Lending

The Shanghai Disneyland milestone is not an isolated case. Every major destination—theme parks, airports, convention centers, stadiums, national parks—has a cumulative attendance record. Most lenders ignore it. That creates an arbitrage opportunity. Funders who integrate CAV into their underwriting can confidently lend to businesses that others deem too volatile. Brokers who bring CAV data to the table strengthen their deal packages. ISOs who target merchants tied to high-CAV anchors build a resilient portfolio.

The old model of chasing year-over-year growth is outdated. The new signal is cumulative. It is stable. It is predictive. And it is sitting in plain sight.
