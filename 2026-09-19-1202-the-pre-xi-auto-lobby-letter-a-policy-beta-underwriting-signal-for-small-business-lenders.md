Six major auto trade groups signed a letter urging the president to bar Chinese automakers from the U.S. ahead of a Xi visit. The headline reads as trade policy. For small business lenders, the useful signal is capital allocation.

## The letter is a market-access signal, not a tariff signal

Trade groups representing major automakers rarely align on much. When they jointly ask the president to bar Chinese automakers ahead of a Xi visit, they are telling lenders that Chinese OEM entry is now a strategic-risk category. Tariffs change landed cost. A bar changes whether the platform exists in the U.S. at all. That difference matters to working capital.

For a dealer, a parts distributor, or a service shop, the revenue tied to a Chinese brand's U.S. launch is not contracted revenue. It is an option on policy. Underwrite it as zero until the policy clears.

## Map the policy-beta exposure

Policy beta is the share of a borrower's cash flow that depends on a regulatory decision. In auto-adjacent small business lending, it shows up in five places.

1. Franchise value. A domestic-brand franchise may gain blue-sky value if Chinese competition is blocked. A Chinese-brand franchise appointment may have no transferable value if the brand cannot distribute. Funders should separate existing franchise cash flow from planned franchise expansion.

2. Floor plan financing. Floor plan lines are inventory-secured, but the collateral is only as good as the market for the VIN. If a Chinese OEM cannot sell in the U.S., model-specific inventory has limited resale. Advance rates on that inventory should be conservative or zero.

3. Parts and accessories. Distributors that pre-stock parts for a brand that never launches carry dead inventory. Distributors that serve the existing installed base carry recurring demand. The underwriting test is installed-base revenue versus launch-dependent revenue.

4. Equipment financing. EV service equipment, diagnostic software, lifts, chargers, and tooling can be brand-specific. If the brand is blocked, the equipment may still have generic use, but the borrower's revenue plan does not. Appraise equipment on generic resale value, not the projected brand volume.

5. Facilities and logistics. Ports, rail, trucking, PDI centers, and dealer real estate adjust to import volume. A policy bar shifts volume but does not eliminate auto logistics. Lenders should monitor concentration by OEM and by port.

## Rewrite the underwriting model

Add a policy-beta screen to auto lending. The screen does not require a political forecast. It requires borrower-level questions.

- Does the borrower have a letter of intent, dealer agreement, or memorandum of understanding with a Chinese OEM?
- Has the borrower ordered brand-specific equipment, parts, or signage?
- What percentage of trailing twelve-month revenue comes from vehicles, parts, or services tied to a brand that is not yet legally cleared for U.S. distribution?
- Can the borrower service other makes with the same labor and equipment?
- What is the resale market for the inventory or equipment if the launch does not happen?

If the answer to the first two questions is yes, treat the related cash flow as speculative. Exclude it from global cash flow. Reduce advance rates. Add covenants that restrict additional brand-specific capex without lender consent. Require monthly reporting on inventory aging by VIN origin and parts category.

For floor plan financing, set eligibility rules by VIN and OEM parent. Do not fund inventory in transit without a landed-cost and resale analysis. For equipment financing, cap advances at generic liquidation value. For working capital loans and merchant cash advance products, monitor deposit patterns for pre-launch spending. A spike in parts purchases or tooling leases before a brand is approved is a warning, not growth.

## What brokers and ISO networks should do

Brokers win by packaging risk, not hiding it. Ask the policy-beta questions during intake. Submit a deal with a clear split: installed-base revenue, domestic OEM revenue, and launch-dependent revenue. Funders will price that better than a generic auto repair or dealer file.

ISO networks can build a vertical-specific application flow. Add fields for OEM agreements, franchise status, brand-specific equipment, and inventory composition. Train agents to flag borrowers that mention a Chinese brand launch in their business plan. That is not an automatic decline. It is a different credit.

Funders should review renewal files. A borrower that looked stable twelve months ago may now have a business plan tied to a policy decision. Auto-renewals can mask that shift. Use the Xi visit and the trade-group letter as a trigger to re-underwrite auto-adjacent exposure.

## Where the opportunity sits

A Chinese automaker bar would not freeze auto lending. It would redirect it. Domestic OEMs and their suppliers would face less import competition. Dealers with domestic franchises could see steadier used-vehicle values and service demand. Independent repair shops that serve the aging fleet gain from longer vehicle hold times. Compliance, cybersecurity, and connected-vehicle software vendors may see more demand as automakers separate Chinese hardware and software from U.S. fleets.

The opportunity is not to pick the policy outcome. It is to finance the parts of the auto ecosystem that do not depend on it. Installed-base service revenue, domestic-brand parts distribution, used-vehicle reconditioning, and generic equipment all have cash flow that survives either outcome. Lenders that can identify those assets will win deals from borrowers that need capital but cannot get a generic auto loan approved.

## The operator takeaway

The auto industry's letter to the president is a trade headline for the press and a policy-beta signal for lenders. Small business lending in the auto channel now requires brand-level and VIN-level underwriting. Funders, brokers, and ISO networks should stop treating auto as one vertical. Split it into installed-base cash flow, domestic franchise cash flow, and policy-dependent launch cash flow. Price each differently. Covenant the last one.

That approach protects collateral, improves revenue quality, and gives brokers a clear story to take to borrowers. In a market where policy can change before a Xi visit ends, the lender that underwrites the option value of a Chinese brand launch as zero will not be the one holding stranded inventory.
