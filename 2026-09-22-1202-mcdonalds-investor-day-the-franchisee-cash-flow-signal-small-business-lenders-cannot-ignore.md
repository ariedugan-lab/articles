# McDonald's Investor Day: The Franchisee Cash-Flow Signal Small Business Lenders Cannot Ignore

McDonald's is hosting an investor day while its U.S. business struggles. For funders, brokers, and ISO networks, the corporate presentation matters less than the operating reality it exposes. The headline is not burger demand. The signal is franchisee free cash flow. When a global QSR brand fights for traffic with value menus, digital offers, and remodels, the franchisee absorbs the cost. Lenders that underwrite the brand instead of the unit will misprice risk.

## The Brand Halo Is Not Collateral

McDonald's corporate can adjust marketing, royalties, and national value platforms. Franchisees operate the restaurants. They pay rent, royalties, ad fund contributions, labor, utilities, insurance, maintenance, technology fees, delivery commissions, and remodel costs. A strong brand does not guarantee a strong borrower. A unit with falling traffic and rising discount mix can post positive sales and negative cash flow. Small business lenders need to treat brand affiliation as a qualifier, not collateral.

The investor day likely surfaces four pressures: U.S. traffic weakness, value-menu economics, digital and delivery mix, and capital requirements for remodels and equipment. Each pressure hits franchisee cash flow differently. Together, they create a new underwriting standard for QSR lending.

## Value Menu Volume Does Not Equal Margin

Value menus drive transactions. They do not automatically drive profit. When McDonald's pushes value deals to recover U.S. traffic, franchisees often fund part of the discount. The check average falls. The cost per transaction remains. Labor, packaging, and occupancy do not shrink with the price.

For funders, the underwriting question is not same-store sales. It is contribution margin per ticket. Pull POS data. Compare discount mix, average check, product mix, and daypart performance. Model a 5 to 10 percent shift toward value items. If debt service coverage falls below 1.25x, the loan structure is too aggressive. For brokers and ISOs, submit deals with a margin bridge. Show gross sales, discount subsidies, cost of goods, labor, and fixed costs. Do not rely on franchisor-reported system sales.

## Digital and Delivery Fees Cut Both Ways

Digital ordering, loyalty apps, kiosks, and delivery aggregators can lift revenue. They also add costs. Delivery commissions can take 15 to 30 percent of the ticket. App promotions train customers to wait for discounts. Kiosks require maintenance and payment processing. Loyalty programs create data but also fund rewards.

Lenders should separate digital sales into channels. In-store, drive-thru, kiosk, app, and delivery have different margins. A franchisee with 40 percent delivery mix is not the same risk as one with 10 percent. Ask for delivery statements, app promo reports, and payment processing statements. Stress test a 200-basis-point increase in aggregator fees. If the unit cannot pass the cost to the customer, cash flow weakens.

## Remodel and Equipment Capex Is a Working Capital Event

QSR brands require periodic remodels, signage updates, kitchen equipment, and technology upgrades. These are not optional. They are license obligations. A franchisee facing a remodel year needs capital before the sales lift arrives. That creates a working capital gap.

Funders can win here by offering equipment financing, lease lines, and short-term working capital tied to the remodel schedule. Brokers and ISOs should package the request with the franchise agreement, remodel letter, contractor bids, and a post-remodel cash-flow projection. Underwrite the gap, not the hope. If the franchisee has three units and two remodels due in 12 months, the consolidated balance sheet may look fine while the operating entity runs dry.

## Multi-Unit Operators Need Unit-Level Underwriting

Multi-unit franchisees often cross-subsidize weak stores. That can hide deterioration. A lender looking at consolidated financials may miss a unit with negative four-wall EBITDA. The fix is unit-level reporting. Request profit and loss statements by location. Review royalty coverage, rent coverage, and fixed-charge coverage by unit. Flag units below 1.0x rent coverage. Flag units with declining traffic for three consecutive quarters.

For funders, concentration limits matter. A portfolio heavy in one brand, one metro, or one franchisee group carries correlated risk. If McDonald's U.S. value strategy compresses margins across a region, those loans deteriorate together. Build exposure caps by brand and by franchisee. Use weekly or biweekly sales reporting. Set covenants tied to royalty coverage and liquidity. If the borrower misses two consecutive reporting cycles, act early.

## Franchisor Support Is Not a Repayment Source

Franchisors sometimes offer rent deferrals, royalty relief, or capex subsidies during downturns. That support helps. It does not replace underwriting. Lenders should model the borrower without assumed relief. If the deal only works when the franchisor defers rent, the deal does not work. Use support as a mitigant, not a repayment source.

Brokers and ISOs should also avoid submitting deals that depend on future franchisee cash flow from an unbuilt remodel or a new value platform. Funders can spot that pattern. It damages the broker relationship. Package the file with a clear use of funds, a primary repayment source, and a secondary source. Show how the borrower handled the last traffic downturn. That history is more valuable than a brand name.

## What Funders, Brokers, and ISO Networks Should Do Now

Funders should update QSR scorecards. Add discount mix, delivery mix, labor cost per hour, rent coverage, remodel obligations, and digital fees. Replace brand-tier pricing with unit-level risk pricing. Tighten documentation requirements. Require POS data and franchisee profit and loss statements.

Brokers and ISOs should build a franchise finance specialty. Learn the FDD. Learn the franchise agreement. Learn the royalty and ad fund structure. Ask for the last four quarters of unit-level P&L. Pre-screen for rent coverage and fixed-charge coverage. Submit cleaner files. The volume will follow.

Merchant cash advance and short-term working capital can bridge a remodel or a seasonal gap. They cannot fix a unit with negative four-wall cash flow. If the borrower needs an advance to pay royalties, that is a warning sign. If the borrower needs capital to renovate a store with strong traffic and a clear payback, that is a financeable event.

McDonald's investor day is a reminder. The U.S. consumer is value-focused. The franchisor will protect the brand. The franchisee will carry the operating cost. Small business lenders that underwrite the unit, not the logo, will price risk correctly. Those that do not will learn the difference between system sales and cash flow.
