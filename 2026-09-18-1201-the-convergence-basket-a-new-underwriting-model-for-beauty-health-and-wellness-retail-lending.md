# The Convergence Basket: A New Underwriting Model for Beauty, Health, and Wellness Retail Lending

The consumer no longer shops beauty, health, and wellness as separate categories. A customer buys a serum, a supplement, a recovery session, and a sleep aid in the same budget cycle. Retailers respond by stacking categories. Salons sell supplements. Gyms sell skincare. Pharmacies add aesthetic services. Med spas sell memberships and retail. The convergence is not a merchandising trend. It changes how cash moves through small businesses.

Most small business lenders still underwrite by category label. They file a borrower under beauty, fitness, pharmacy, or healthcare. That structure misses the real risk. The borrower's revenue quality depends on the blended basket, not the NAICS code. Funders, brokers, and ISO networks that adjust now will price better and originate more volume.

## One wallet, multiple revenue lines

The convergence creates one consumer budget with multiple merchant touchpoints. A borrower can show strong top-line growth while mix shifts toward lower-margin or higher-liability revenue. A salon that adds supplements may grow sales but dilute gross margin. A med spa that sells prepaid treatment packages may collect cash today but owe service delivery for months. A gym that adds recovery services may increase utilization and labor cost. A pharmacy that adds beauty may carry more SKUs and working capital.

For funders, the first underwriting move is to segment revenue. Do not accept a single monthly deposit figure. Break the borrower into three revenue types: replenishment, service, and recurring access. Replenishment includes skincare, supplements, and consumables. Service includes treatments, IV therapy, recovery, and aesthetic procedures. Recurring access includes memberships, subscriptions, and prepaid packages.

Each type has a different cash conversion cycle. Replenishment is card-settled and repeatable. Service is booking-driven and labor-heavy. Recurring access is prepaid but creates deferred revenue. A blended business can look healthy on bank statements while carrying a liability that does not appear in the deposit line.

## The cash conversion split

Underwrite the split, not the total. If replenishment is more than 60 percent of revenue, focus on inventory turns and repeat purchase rates. If service is more than 40 percent, focus on booking utilization, provider capacity, and no-show rates. If recurring access is more than 25 percent, isolate deferred revenue and measure redemption.

Prepaid wellness packages are not free cash. They are a promise. A borrower that sells 500 IV drips at a discount must deliver 500 treatments. That requires clinician time, supplies, and space. If the borrower sells beyond fulfillment capacity, the cash becomes a working capital trap. Funders should calculate deferred revenue divided by monthly fulfillment capacity. If the result exceeds 90 days, flag the deal or reduce advance.

Membership churn matters more than gross membership count. A wellness retailer with 2,000 active members and 12 percent monthly churn has a different risk profile than one with 800 members and 3 percent churn. The first must acquire constantly to stand still. The second has durable cash flow. Brokers should provide membership software exports with churn, utilization, and average tenure.

## Inventory and equipment risk

Convergence pushes merchants to carry wider assortments. A wellness retailer may stock skincare, supplements, recovery tools, and medical-grade products. Inventory financing should not treat all wellness inventory equal. Set advance rates by SKU velocity and supplier return terms. Supplements and skincare often turn. Devices and aesthetic equipment depreciate. Consumables tied to a specific device can become stranded if the device breaks or demand fades.

Equipment lending needs utilization data. A med spa buying a laser or cryotherapy unit should show booking demand, provider training, and service contracts. The device invoice is not collateral. The cash flow from treatments is. Funders should underwrite device payback based on realistic weekly treatment volume, not manufacturer projections.

Working capital lines should match the inventory cycle. If a borrower buys supplements monthly, a 90-day line can work. If the borrower buys seasonal beauty inventory, repayment should follow the sell-through curve. A fixed daily payment can drain cash before the inventory converts.

## The data stack for convergence lending

Lenders need more than bank statements and a credit score. The convergence basket requires five data sources. First, POS and payment processor data for category-level sales, refunds, and chargebacks. Second, inventory management data for sell-through, weeks on hand, and supplier terms. Third, booking and membership software for active members, churn, utilization, and deferred revenue. Fourth, bank data for payroll, rent, supplier payments, and tax liabilities. Fifth, compliance data for licenses, insurance, and claim substantiation.

Brokers and ISO networks can win by packaging these documents upfront. A complete file moves faster and prices better. A file with only bank statements forces the funder to guess. Guessing leads to declines or risk-based pricing that kills the deal.

## Revenue quality tests

Run six tests on every convergence deal. One, repeat rate. What percentage of customers return within 60 or 90 days? Two, attachment rate. What percentage buy across two or more categories? Cross-category customers usually have higher lifetime value, but they also have higher expectation risk if outcomes fall short. Three, refund and chargeback rate by category. Supplements and skincare can spike when influencer claims outrun product performance. Four, membership churn and utilization. Unlimited packages with high utilization can crush margin. Five, deferred revenue ratio. Prepaid packages divided by monthly fulfillment capacity. Six, concentration. A single hero SKU or influencer-driven demand spike can reverse quickly.

These tests change advance rates and repayment structure. A replenishment-heavy borrower can support a higher advance. A service-heavy borrower needs more cushion for labor and no-shows. A subscription-heavy borrower can support longer terms if churn is low. A prepaid-heavy borrower may need escrowed reserves or milestone releases.

## Product design implications

Funders should build a blended working capital product. Draw periods tied to inventory buys. Repayment that combines fixed ACH with card receivables sweep. Payment dates aligned to recurring billing cycles. For membership-heavy businesses, avoid stacking merchant cash advance on top of high deferred revenue unless the funder can reconcile daily card settlements against service delivery.

Brokers should match the product to the revenue type. Use equipment financing for device purchases. Use revenue-based financing for service-heavy med spas. Use inventory lines for retail-heavy wellness shops. Use term loans for buildouts only when membership or service contracts support the debt service.

ISO networks should create vertical playbooks. Train reps to ask for booking data, membership reports, and SKU-level sell-through. Score leads by convergence profile. A replenishment-heavy wellness shop is a different lead than a prepaid med spa. The pitch, the documents, and the funder panel should differ.

## Risk flags

Flag prepaid treatment packages sold at deep discounts with no capacity plan. Flag high employee turnover among licensed providers. Flag inventory build without sell-through. Flag aggressive influencer marketing with unsubstantiated claims. Flag cash-heavy operations with weak card settlement. Flag multiple locations with shared membership liabilities. Flag any borrower that cannot separate deferred revenue from earned revenue.

## The opportunity

Beauty, health, and wellness are converging into one retail category. That creates a new small business lending vertical. It is not beauty. It is not fitness. It is not healthcare. It is a blended cash-flow business. Lenders that underwrite the blended basket can price risk more accurately and approve more good deals. Brokers that package the right data can move files faster. ISO networks that train on convergence can capture commissions from a growing merchant base.

The category is converging. Underwriting must converge too. Stop funding labels. Fund the basket, the cadence, and the deferred liability.
