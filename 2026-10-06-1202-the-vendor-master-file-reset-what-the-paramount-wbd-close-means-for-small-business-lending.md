## The close is a procurement event

Paramount's takeover of Warner Bros. Discovery closes. Most coverage focuses on content, streaming, and debt. For small business lenders, the signal is in accounts payable. A merger of this size triggers a vendor master file reset. That reset changes payment timing, contract assignability, and revenue quality for production vendors, post-production shops, equipment rental houses, local media buyers, and agencies.

Funders, brokers, and ISO networks should treat the close as a working capital event. It does not create immediate credit risk for every media-adjacent small business. It does create a 90-to-180-day underwriting window where payment behavior can break from historical norms.

## Three shocks inside the first two quarters

1. Vendor master consolidation.
Paramount and WBD maintain separate supplier records. After close, procurement teams merge vendor lists. Duplicate vendors face renegotiation or elimination. A small business that invoices both entities may see one vendor code deactivated. That can halt purchase orders and delay receivables. Lenders that underwrite on trailing deposits will miss the change until cash flow drops.

2. Payment-term reset.
Mergers create leverage. The combined entity can extend net terms from 30 to 60 or 90 days. It can also move vendors to a new AP portal. Both changes stretch the cash conversion cycle for small suppliers. A borrower with healthy revenue can still miss a merchant cash advance payment because receivables arrive three weeks later. Underwriting must separate revenue quality from payment timing.

3. PO freeze and rebid.
Integration teams freeze discretionary spend while they review contracts. Production services, events, and marketing projects can pause. Vendors with high customer concentration face a revenue air pocket. They still carry payroll, rent, insurance, and equipment leases. That is a classic working capital gap.

## What funders must re-underwrite

Do not treat media as one risk bucket. Segment borrowers by end customer and contract structure.

- End-customer concentration. If more than 20% of revenue comes from Paramount, WBD, or their pass-through agencies, flag the file. If more than 40% comes from both combined, require a current PO pipeline and a receivables aging report.
- Contract assignability. Check change-of-control clauses. Some contracts terminate or require consent after a merger. A vendor with a strong backlog can lose it if the contract is not assignable.
- Direct versus pass-through revenue. Direct vendors may get renegotiated. Agencies and subcontractors may get squeezed further down the chain. Both carry different dispute risk.
- Receivables aging. Watch for a shift from 0-30 to 61-90 days. Dispute codes and short pays can signal a vendor master issue before a full default.
- Bank data frequency. Monthly statements are too slow. Funders using cash flow underwriting should pull weekly or daily data during the integration window. Look for AP portal migration, changed remittance advice, and partial payments.
- Fixed-cost coverage. Calculate how many weeks the borrower can operate if one major customer pauses payments. If the answer is less than six weeks, the file needs a shorter duration, smaller advance, or a holdback structure.

## Implications for funders and portfolio managers

Reprice or restructure renewals for borrowers with direct Paramount-WBD exposure. A merchant cash advance renewal based on pre-close volume can overstate current capacity. Use a lower collection factor for the next two quarters. Consider split funding or revenue-based advances tied to verified receivables.

Tighten monitoring triggers. A 15% drop in weekly deposits from a media customer should trigger a review. Do not wait for two missed payments. The integration period rewards early intervention.

Build a watchlist by NAICS code and customer name. Production services, audio post, color, VFX, equipment rental, casting, location services, catering, and transportation are first-order exposures. Advertising and media buying are second-order exposures. Local media outlets and cinema concession vendors are third-order exposures. Each has a different lag between the close and cash impact.

## Implications for brokers and ISO networks

Brokers and ISO networks originate a large share of small business lending in this segment. Package the file with integration context. Lenders will price uncertainty if they see it. They will decline if they discover it after funding.

Ask five questions on every media-adjacent deal:
1. Which legal entity pays the borrower?
2. Is the contract assignable after a merger?
3. What percentage of revenue comes from Paramount, WBD, or their agencies?
4. Has the borrower received a vendor master update, new AP portal notice, or term change?
5. What is the current accounts receivable aging compared with 90 days ago?

If the borrower cannot answer, treat the file as higher risk. If the borrower has diversified customers and direct contracts outside the merged entity, the close may create opportunity. Integration teams often cut large incumbents and hire smaller specialists for speed. Post-close vendors with clean contracts and diversified revenue can qualify for growth capital.

## The opportunity after the reset

The first 90 days are risky. The next 12 months are opportunistic. Merged media companies need production capacity, local marketing, and specialized post-production. They will consolidate some vendors and expand others. Lenders that track the vendor master reset can identify survivors and fund them before competitors.

Focus on borrowers with:
- Two or more end customers, none above 25% of revenue.
- Contracts that survived change-of-control review.
- Positive accounts receivable aging in the last 60 days.
- Recurring or retainer-based revenue.
- Equipment or assets that support other media customers.

Those borrowers can absorb a payment-term change. They can also take on new projects when the combined entity rebids work.

## Bottom line

The Paramount-WBD close is not only a content story. It is a vendor payment story. Funders, brokers, and ISO networks that underwrite the post-close vendor reset will avoid surprise defaults. They will also find small business lending opportunities among suppliers that survive consolidation. Pull current receivables data, verify contract assignability, and reprice concentration risk before the first AP cycle under the new entity.
