# The Estate Gift KYC Bottleneck: A New Underwriting Signal for Small Business Lenders Serving Nonprofits

Financial institutions are delaying charitable IRA distributions after a donor dies. The stated reason is compliance. They want personal information on charity employees before releasing funds. Nonprofits call it a bottleneck. Small business lenders should call it a cash-flow timing signal.

The story is not only about charities. It is about any small business that depends on estate-linked revenue. Funeral homes, estate law practices, planned-giving consultants, cemeteries, senior care operators, religious organizations, food banks, thrift retailers, and donor-advised fund sponsors all sit near this money. When the gift stalls, their working capital needs do not.

## What changed

An IRA charitable distribution, often a qualified charitable distribution, moves from a custodian to a charity. When the donor is alive, the process is known. When the donor dies, the custodian must verify the beneficiary, the estate, and the charity. Many financial firms now apply KYC and AML rules to the receiving charity. That can mean collecting personal information on charity employees, board members, or authorized signers.

Charities push back. They may not have the data. They may not want to hand over employee personal information. The custodian may not release the gift until the file is complete. The result is a receivable that looks certain on paper and behaves like a slow, conditional payment in practice.

For a small business lender, that is the underwriting issue. A pledge, a beneficiary designation, or a letter from an estate attorney is not cash. It is a claim with a compliance process attached.

## Why this matters to funders

Nonprofit lending and nonprofit-adjacent small business lending have a cash-flow pattern. Revenue arrives in lumps. Grants, annual campaigns, and estate gifts do not match monthly payroll, rent, insurance, and program costs. A delayed estate gift can force a charity to cut services, delay vendor payments, or seek a business line of credit.

That creates demand. It also creates risk. Funders that treat expected estate gifts as near-cash will over-advance. Funders that treat them as conditional receivables can price the delay and win quality deals.

The same logic applies to for-profit vendors. A funeral home may carry receivables from estates. An estate attorney may wait on probate before final payment. A cemetery may finance a mausoleum project against future bequests. A senior care operator may depend on charitable subsidies. A planned-giving consultant may work on contingency. Each business has a different link to the estate pipeline, but each can face a working capital gap when a custodian slows a distribution.

## Build an estate-gift underwriting screen

Do not underwrite the headline gift. Underwrite the release process. Use a four-part screen.

First, identify the custodian. Some financial firms release charitable distributions quickly. Others require extensive charity KYC. Ask which institution holds the IRA. Ask whether the charity has a pre-cleared compliance packet. Ask how many estate gifts the charity has received from that custodian in the last 24 months. Past release times are a better signal than any attorney estimate.

Second, document the claim. You want the beneficiary designation, death certificate, letters testamentary or letters of administration, estate tax clearance if applicable, and written confirmation from the custodian that the file is under review. A letter of intent from an estate is not collateral. A beneficiary designation with a clean custodian file is stronger, but it is still not cash.

Third, measure concentration. If one estate gift exceeds 10% of the borrower's annual operating revenue, treat it as a concentration risk. If it exceeds 25%, treat it as a single-event risk. Use a lower advance rate. Shorten the term. Add a covenant that ties additional draws to receipt of funds.

Fourth, price the delay. Build a timeline by custodian and state. Probate can take months. KYC review can add weeks. Charities often budget on expected receipt dates that are too optimistic. Underwrite to the slowest reasonable date, not the fastest.

## Product design for funders, brokers, and ISO networks

Funders should offer a bridge product, not a blank check. A short-duration working capital line with interest-only payments can fit. A revenue-based advance secured by diversified charitable receipts can work if the advance rate is conservative. An MCA against a pending estate gift is a poor fit. It stacks a high-frequency repayment schedule on a low-frequency, uncertain receipt.

Brokers and ISO networks can build a niche here. Most reps do not ask about estate gift pipelines. Train them to ask three questions:

1. What percentage of your annual revenue comes from estate gifts or bequests?
2. Which financial institution holds the IRA or donor-advised fund?
3. Have you completed the receiving charity's KYC and AML packet?

Those answers change the file. A nonprofit with a diversified donor base and a pre-cleared custodian relationship is a better risk than one waiting on a single seven-figure estate gift.

Underwriters should require a receipt-based repayment trigger. If possible, set up a sweep from the charity's operating account or a directed payment from the custodian. Do not rely on the borrower to forward funds manually. Use a lockbox or a controlled disbursement account for large expected gifts.

## The compliance friction is not going away

Financial firms face real KYC and AML obligations. Charities face real privacy and administrative limits. The conflict will produce more delays, not fewer. Lenders can reduce friction by helping borrowers prepare a standard charity KYC package. That package can include formation documents, IRS determination letter, W-9, board list, authorized signers, and a privacy policy. It does not remove the custodian's request for employee data, but it speeds the file.

Lenders can also track release times by custodian. Build a matrix. Name the institutions that release quickly and those that do not. Share that matrix with underwriters and ISO reps. Over time, it becomes a proprietary underwriting asset.

## The opportunity

The lending opportunity is not to bypass compliance. It is to finance the gap that compliance creates. Nonprofits and their vendors need working capital when estate gifts stall. Funders that understand the release process can originate loans with clear repayment sources. Brokers and ISO networks can own a niche that most competitors ignore. Underwriters can avoid the trap of treating a promised gift as cash.

The next time a borrower presents a bequest, a beneficiary designation, or an estate attorney letter, ask a different question. Not how much is coming. Ask who controls the release, what the KYC file requires, and when the custodian last paid a charity like this one. That is the new underwriting signal in small business lending.
